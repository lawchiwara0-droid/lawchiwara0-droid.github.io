About Lawson Chiwara

Lawson Chiwara is a Zimbabwean O-Level English educator, writer, and freelance community journalist based in Zaka, Masvingo Province. Drawing on classroom teaching experience in rural schools and ground-level reporting across the Lowveld, his work focuses on bridging educational access gaps and documenting rural community development.

His classroom pedagogy—combining Shona-to-English conceptual bridges, low Teacher Talking Time (TTT), and structured writing templates—helped lift rural Form 4 English pass rates by 15% and inspired his flagship remedial workbook, English Intervention: O-Level Exam Survival Toolkit.

English Intervention: O-Level Exam Survival Toolkit

By Lawson Chiwara • Remedial Workbook & Self-Study Programme (Paper 1 & Paper 2)

Designed specifically for struggling, rural, and self-studying O-Level candidates, this structured intervention programme moves learners step by step from familiar local contexts (El Niño drought resilience, mukambo entrepreneurship, rural clinics, and anti-drug awareness) up to full 50-mark ZIMSEC exam simulations.

  • 6 Progressive Units + 2 Readiness Checkpoints: Fades scaffolds systematically from 100% guided frames to an 800-word unfamiliar regional comprehension text.
  • Parallel Paper 1 Writing Ladder: Builds composition skills from single sentences and functional connectors to 380+ word annotated models and live rough-draft walkthroughs.
  • Authentic Exam Mechanics: Passage-anchored Section B drills, 60- to 160-word boxed summary grids, 20-point marking keys, and a 5-step Personal Error Log.

Zaka North • Local Governance & Education

Vacate Schools Boundaries — Chief Ndanga Gives Ultimatum to Residents

By Lawson Chiwara • Lowveld Checkpoint

ZAKA NORTH — Chief Ndanga has issued a firm warning to villagers occupying land within areas reserved for schools and other public institutions, as traditional leaders and council officials move to enforce a 24-metre institutional boundary buffer in line with local development plans.

The warning follows growing tension in the Ndanga area, where several households have residential structures close to institutions including Danda Primary and Secondary Schools and are resisting directives to give way to land reserved for institutional development. Previous misunderstandings had emerged between affected residents and school authorities over plot boundaries.

Zaka North • Rural Development & Employment

Danda Quarry Project Creates Jobs and Development in Zaka

By Lawson Chiwara • Lowveld Checkpoint

DANDA VILLAGE — Mahwenje Mining plans to invest about US$2 million in a new quarry project in Danda Village, Zaka North. The development aims to create local jobs and support infrastructure improvements in nearby communities.

The project, which has received approval from the Environmental Management Agency (EMA), Zaka Rural District Council, traditional leaders, and residents after consultations, is now in the site-mapping stage.
